NLP Research Pilot

Does Reasoning Make AI
Overconfident Near Its Knowledge Boundary?

A temporal calibration study using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B


Model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (fp16)  ·  Platform: Kaggle GPU T4 ×2 (free)  ·  N = 50 questions × 2 modes

The Problem

Every AI language model is trained on data up to a cutoff date. After that, it knows nothing new.

But data about very recent events (just before the cutoff) is sparse — the internet hasn't had time to write extensively about them yet.

So the model might know recent events but not realise how shaky that knowledge is — and express false confidence.

Reasoning (chain-of-thought) could make this worse: the model talks itself into an answer.

Research Hypothesis

"A reasoning-mode LLM will become more overconfident on factual questions as those events approach its training cutoff — more so than the same model without reasoning."

Thinking ON

Chain-of-thought reasoning before answering. Hypothesis: MORE overconfident near cutoff.

Thinking OFF

Direct answer, no reasoning. Hypothesis: LESS affected by temporal proximity.

Metric

Overconfidence gap = stated confidence − actual accuracy × 100

Pipeline

4
Compute overconfidence gap per bucket per mode
5
Plot gap vs temporal distance — look for trend

Key Finding

What we expected

Overconfidence (gap > 0) rising toward the cutoff, especially for Thinking ON.


What we found

Both modes are systematically underconfident (gap always negative). The model says "5% sure" but is right 50% of the time.


However, Thinking ON is less underconfident than OFF, and its gap rises toward the cutoff — the gradient is in the right direction.

Limitations & What a Full Paper Needs

Current limitations


  • N = 50 questions — too small for statistical significance
  • Single model family — no cross-model claims
  • Verbalized confidence is noisy — self-reported numbers ≠ real probabilities
  • Thinking OFF is methodologically awkward — R1 is RL-trained to always reason
  • Training cutoff is approximate ("~July 2024")

Summary

  • Tested whether reasoning makes AI overconfident near its training cutoff
  • Used DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, 50 questions across 5 temporal buckets
  • Found systematic underconfidence — opposite of hypothesis direction
  • BUT: Thinking ON is less underconfident near the cutoff (mild temporal gradient)
  • Signal strength: 13.6 pp → "scale up to confirm"
  • Full pipeline built, validated, and free to run on Kaggle

Bottom line for the paper

Reasoning mode does shift confidence toward the cutoff — but the absolute direction is underconfidence, not overconfidence. A larger study with log-prob confidence would resolve whether this is a real calibration effect or a verbalization artifact.
